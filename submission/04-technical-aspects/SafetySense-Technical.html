On-device intelligence, a small server, real calls

The iPhone does all the sensing and deciding. A minimal Supabase backend keeps a heartbeat, calls contacts through Twilio, and hands off to dispatch through the Noonlight API.

The risk engine (our code)

Each signal adds weighted points that fade over time. Weights depend on the mode (Night Out, Date, Walk, Ride) and the time of day, and the user picks Relaxed, Balanced or Vigilant sensitivity. The engine is deterministic and unit-tested, and every escalation carries a plain-language reason ("Scream, then no movement"). A "playful context" rule halves shouting when laughter or cheering is clearly present, which cuts party false alarms.

04Key flows

A scream, then silence

  1. 0 sSoundAnalysis flags screaming. Then CoreMotion sees stillness.
  2. The risk engine crosses its threshold, and a Level 1 check-in starts with a haptic and the Live Activity.
  3. +45 sNo answer. Level 2: the app seals the 30 s buffer as evidence and calls alert/create.
  4. The server calls and texts each contact through Twilio. Contacts open the live link.
  5. +90 sNo PIN. The watchdog or the app escalates to Noonlight, which calls the user and then dispatches.

The phone goes dark

  1. During a session the app sends a heartbeat every 60 s. Missed sends wait in an offline outbox.
  2. The phone is smashed, switched off or put in airplane mode, and heartbeats stop without "End session".
  3. After the grace period, pg_cron's watchdog creates a Level 2 heartbeat_lost alert at the last known location.
  4. Contacts are called, and dispatch follows after 90 s unless a contact resolves it.
  5. Evidence uploaded before the phone went dark is already safe on the server, encrypted.

05Engineering quality

  • Swift 6 language mode with complete strict concurrency, SwiftUI, and an XcodeGen project. The build has zero warnings.
  • Protocol-based services (sensing, risk, escalation, backend, vault) with demo and mock versions, so tests and demo videos never touch the network or place calls.
  • 136 Swift Testing tests covering the risk engine, detectors, escalation timing, the duress path, vault encryption and tamper checks, and the backend contract. A shell smoke test runs the whole server flow in dry-run mode.
  • Resilience: an offline outbox for heartbeats and alert locations, one live alert per session (repeat calls reuse it), and server-side escalation that doesn't depend on the phone surviving.
