## What's in the file

- **Cover**: title frame. It's set as the file thumbnail if Figma kept the change; check the file browser.
- **Flow overview**: every screen as a thumbnail, with tap arrows and auto-advance arrows across eight lanes (plus 20b and 20c beside 20): onboarding, core, date + contact view, Walk Home + Ride, tabs + alert timeline, the three promise cards, the per-mode check-ins, and a PIN typed on the countdown (13e, 19e, 20e).
- **Prototype**: 48 iPhone screens (402×874) plus 8 overlays. Device: iPhone 17 Pro. Every visible button, chip and tab does something; nothing dead-ends.
  - Flows / starting points: `1 · Full demo (start here)`, `2 · Night Out → Alert (skip onboarding)`, `3 · Date mode`, `4 · Contact live link`, `5 · Walk Home and Ride modes`.
  - Start screen: "Start the prototype" runs the full demo. The flow chips (Onboarding, Night Out, Check-in, Alert, Duress PIN, Date mode, Contact live link, Support, Walk Home, Ride) jump straight to that part.
  - Onboarding: Start → Welcome → Three promises (three cards: Next → Next → Sounds good) → Trusted contacts → Permissions → Two PINs → Practice drill → (Run the drill shows the TEST result) → Home. The steps are labelled 2 to 5 of 5 and the five-segment progress bar matches (it fills across the three promise cards). On Permissions, each Allow flips that row to On; Allow all turns both on and continues, and once both rows are On the main button reads Continue (Not now disappears). The Safety PIN box starts empty; tapping either box opens a PIN pad, and any key fills that box's four dots ("PIN set") and closes the pad. Settings later shows "Safety & duress PINs: On". The back chevron goes back. Skip on the three promise cards goes to Trusted contacts (choosing who to call is the one required step); Skip on later steps goes to Home.
  - Home: the Walk Home, Night Out, Date and Ride chips all switch modes instantly (the selected chip, heading and Start Guard label change) and remember the mode. Edit or the contacts card opens Trusted contacts. The tab bar opens Contacts, Vault, Support and Settings, and their Home tab returns to the Home screen of the mode you picked. Support and Settings scroll to the end of the real app's list (stitched simulator screenshots) under a fixed tab bar; every hotline button, Open my Vault and "In danger right now? Call 911" do something. The app's internal Developer row is hidden on Settings.
  - Core (Night Out): Home → Start Guard → Guard active (0:06 elapsed, next check-in 2:10 PM) → (auto-advances after 4 s) Check-in (the seconds count down from 20) → "I'm okay" goes to Guard · checked in (20:12 elapsed, next check-in 2:30 PM). "I need help", or no answer for 20 s, goes to Alert countdown (counts down from 15, and restarts at 15 on every visit; Maya gets a call and a text; Jordan and Sam are texted) → (Send help now, or 15 s) Alert active → I'm safe now → Enter PIN → Safety PIN → Marked safe → Find support → Support → Home. "Back to home" on both Marked safe screens returns to the Home screen of the mode the alert came from (Walk Home, Night Out, Date or Ride). Both Guard screens have a visible End Guard button. Alert active scrolls: below the map are all three contacts (Maya answered the call, Jordan and Sam got texts) and the alert timeline; the dock has I'm safe now and Call 911 (opens the no-call note). On the countdown and on Enter PIN only the number keys count as a PIN; backspace does nothing (there is nothing to delete). "Not now" on Enter PIN, opened from the countdown, restarts the countdown at 15; opened from Alert active it goes back to the alert. A PIN typed on the countdown, before dispatch, leads to 13e ("ALERT 2:11 · STOPPED BEFORE DISPATCH") or, for the duress PIN, 19e and then Maya's page 20e with a 2:11 PM cancel; a PIN from Alert active keeps the 2:11–2:31 summary on 13/19. On 20e the timeline is newest first (2:12 dispatch requested above the 2:11 duress cancel) and the page header says Updated 2:12:10 PM. The Risk card, status chips, the whole arrival card and Vault clip rows open the prototype note; the whole Ride details card opens the Ride sealed-details note (O2r: Uber, Silver Camry, BXR 482, driver Daniel).
  - Alert context: the alert remembers which Guard you were in and why it started (no answer, or "I need help"), using prototype variables. The countdown's "Why" line, the mode chip on Alert active, the summary on Marked safe ("RIDE · ALERT 2:11–2:31 · 3 CLIPS IN VAULT") and the contact's live link (reason line plus that mode's sealed details: route and ETA, plate and driver, or the date's name) all follow it. Night Out with "no answer" is the default.
  - Walk Home: Home → Walk Home → Start Guard → Walk setup (destination + ETA; Maya follows along, and all three contacts are alerted if you don't arrive) → Start walking → Guard · Walk Home → "I made it · End Guard" goes Home. The pink pill ("Simulate a long stop") or the ring opens Walk Home's own check-in; "I'm okay" returns to the walk with a quiet confirmation and the arrival check still counting down, and "I need help" (or no answer) starts a Walk Home alert.
  - Ride: Home → Ride → Start Guard → Ride setup (service, plate, driver, car) → Start Ride guard → Guard · Ride → "I've arrived · End Guard" goes Home. The pill ("Simulate an unexpected stop") or the ring opens Ride's own check-in; "I'm okay" returns to the ride, and an alert from here shows the plate and driver on the live link.
  - Date mode: Home → Date → Start Guard → Date setup (name and optional photo, sealed) → Start Date guard → Date guard. After "I'm okay" the Date guard shows the time moving on (52:14 elapsed, next check-in 4:10 PM). The sealed card ("Chris"; the photo stays on the phone) previews what contacts would see only if an alert fires; the ring or the pink "Prototype · Simulate a check-in" pill jumps to Date mode's own check-in, and "I'm okay" returns to the Date guard (never to Night Out); End Date guard goes Home. Fields on the three setup sheets open the prototype note. Every check-in starts at 20 and the dispatch countdown at 15 on every visit (the prototype resets component state when it opens those screens).
  - Duress branch: Enter PIN → Duress PIN → Marked safe (looks identical apart from a pink prototype pill) → (6 s, or tap the pill) the contact's live link: "Still active. The cancel on Alex's phone at 2:31 PM used their duress PIN, so help is still coming", with a 2:31 PM timeline entry → Restart demo. If Maya had already tapped "I'm on my way" or "I called 911" on her live link, this page is 20b or 20c instead: the same page with her 2:13 PM response kept in the timeline.
  - Contact live link (web): Alert active → Share link (or the Your people card) → scrollable live-link page (Maya's view, with an iOS status bar). Under the reason line the dispatch note follows straight on. Maya hasn't responded yet; "I'm on my way" / "I called 911" switch back to Alex's alert (11b, 11c) showing that response in the people list and timeline; "Back to Alex's phone" returns to Alert active as it was: 11 if Maya hasn't answered, 11b or 11c if she has. After Maya responds, her button on 18 shows "✓ You're on your way · Alex can see it" (or "✓ You called 911"), and 20b/20c show the same confirmed state. Her own 2:13 PM response is also added to the top of 18's "What's happened" list. Dispatch is already requested, so the page says so instead of offering Send help now. Open in Maps, the map zoom buttons and the call-911 link open explanatory overlays. On all live-link pages the prototype pill is the same 44 px pill in a tall solid bottom bar, so it never covers page content, and the page scrolls far enough for the footer to clear it.
  - Contacts tab: tapping a contact row (or its ⋯ menu) opens the prototype note. "What your contacts see" opens an example live link marked "Preview · example" (nothing is happening); its buttons explain that nothing is sent, the sealed-details box says it is filled in for Walk Home, Date and Ride, and the pill goes back to Contacts.
  - Overlays: the Date guard's sealed card opens O2 ("Chris"), the Ride guard's opens O2r (ride, plate, driver); hotline and 911 buttons open "This prototype never places calls" (no tel: links anywhere); the PIN boxes open a PIN pad; buttons on the contact preview say nothing is sent; other app-only controls open a short prototype note (worded for both the app and the contacts' web page). Close, or tap outside the card.
- **Components**: two ticking countdown component sets (check-in 20→0, dispatch 15→0, one second per variant), a Button component set (Pink / Teal / Coral / Ghost, with Label text, Icon swap and Show icon properties) and 11 icon components.

Screen fills are the real app screenshots from `design/screens/`. The live-link pages were rendered from `web/a.html` with
fictional demo data on a consistent timeline (alert 2:11 PM, contacts called and texted 2:11 PM, dispatch 2:12 PM, Maya responds 2:13 PM, cancelled 2:31 PM; the Vault's three clips are 2:11:21, 2:11:51 and 2:12:21 PM). The iOS status-bar clock on every screen follows the same story (onboarding 1:45–1:48, guards from 1:50, check-ins 2:10–2:11, alert 2:11–2:13, marked safe 2:31). Real Figma layers sit on top:
buttons (instances of the Button component), the "I'm safe now" dock, the PIN picker, filled form fields, small text patches that
keep times and names consistent, toasts, the prototype nav pills, and invisible hotspots. The Start screen and both Marked safe
screens are built entirely from Figma layers.
