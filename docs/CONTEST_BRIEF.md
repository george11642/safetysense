## Mission (what they are judging)

The goal is "useful applications for the community" that "improve an experience for our students and beyond."

## ⚠️ Key things to know
1. **They want a prototype link, not a coded app.** FAQ: "Can I submit a coded app instead of a prototype? **No.** You may start with a coded app, but … you will need to submit a prototype and pitch deck." The required item is a **link to a high-fidelity prototype** (Figma is recommended; set sharing to "anyone with the link can view"). A real iOS build is extra credibility, not a replacement for the prototype.
2. **Presentation Day doesn't allow your own device.** FAQ: "Outside computers/devices are not allowed to connect to the UNM Rainforest system." The live demo has to be **slides with screenshots and embedded pre-recorded video**. A downloadable demo won't be used on stage, but it helps with judge credibility and Q&A.
3. **The app must be unpublished.** An eligible entry is "a prototype and demonstration of a novel idea for a **currently unpublished** application." **Do not release it on the App Store before the contest is over.** TestFlight or a simulator build is fine.
4. **Each required document is a separate PDF or Word attachment.** Don't send links. The only link in the email is the prototype.
5. **Bring unofficial transcripts on paper.** If we're a finalist, each team member brings a **hard-copy unofficial transcript** to the mandatory practice session. **Do not email them.**
6. **No resubmissions.** An app pitched in an earlier year's contest can't be entered again.
7. **IP stays with us.** They can use our names, images, and screenshots for marketing.
8. Extra-credit sessions (Sep 17 kickoff, Oct 8 and Oct 20 mentoring) are over. George's attendance isn't recorded. **Ask George.** Each is worth up to 5 points, with a 10-point maximum.

## Technical submission (due Nov 6, 11:59 pm)
**To:** itevents@unm.edu
**Subject:** `App Contest Technical Submission – <Team or App Name>`. The Helpful page shows a variant, "App Contest Submission – <Team or App Name>". Use the Technical Submission wording.

Required attachments (each a PDF or Word file, never a link):
1. **Short bio** of the developers/team.
2. **3 screenshots** of the app. These may be used for score-card identification or promotion.
3. **Purpose, audience, functions, pathway to satisfaction**
4. **Proposed technical aspects**: technical components (APIs, feeds, libraries, models, third-party services) **plus an architecture diagram** (Visio, Lucidchart, Excalidraw, etc.; a legible screenshot is OK)
5. **Security**: security considerations around private data and sensitive information. For our app this matters a lot, and one judge is a UNM Data Privacy & Compliance Specialist.
6. **Prototype link** (Figma or similar), set to "anyone with the link can view." It must show the user flows and the main use case.

## Presentation (finalists)
- Email the deck by **Nov 16 at noon** with subject `App Contest Presentation Submission – <Team or App Name>`.
- **8 minutes** presenting (strictly timed, with no overrun into Q&A) and **4 minutes** of judge Q&A.
- Spend 1–2 minutes on the team (name, institution, year, area of study), the app, the problem it addresses, and the technology. Go slowly enough for an audience that has never seen the app.
- Spend the rest on the pitch and the "wow" factor. Narrate the slides, and feel free to use screenshots or embedded pre-recorded video.
- The deck runs on **their** machine. Use a portable format (PPTX or Google Slides with embedded MP4s, plus a PDF backup). Figma Slides is possible but riskier offline.
